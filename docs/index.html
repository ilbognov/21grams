AI design, with a soul.

21 Grams makes what your AI agent designs stop looking AI-made. Each skill teaches the agent a working designer's taste, so its screens, flows and video edits feel like real design, made by someone who cared.

npx skills add ilbognov/21grams

The idea

In 1907 a doctor claimed that the human soul weighs 21 grams. The science didn't hold up, but the idea stuck: there's something light and hard to measure that makes a thing alive. That's the little ghost in the logo.

Design has its own 21 grams. A screen can have every component in the right place and still feel dead. What's missing is small: the spacing rhythm, the easing curve, a button that answers when you tap it, copy that sounds like a person, the one decision nobody would make by default.

Code and video agents are now very good at the first 99%. They build a working screen or a rough cut in minutes. They're also trained on everything, so left alone they produce the most likely answer: purple gradients, emoji headings, three equal cards, captions that all look the same. The work looks AI-made because it is the statistical middle.

21 Grams skills put the missing weight back. They're written by a designer, from real rules and real examples, and they make the agent check its work against them before you see it.

What makes them different

Opinionated

Every rule is a decision someone made and can defend. Nothing is generic best practice padding.

Your system

Design skills work through your project's own components and tokens. They never add a package.

Shown, not told

Each skill page has a before and after, so you can see what changes before you install.

Hi, I'm Ilija

I'm Ilija Bogdanovic, founding design engineer at Conquer AI. UI and UX have been my thing since I started designing, and these days I design and build with an AI agent in the loop.

That's fast, and most of the time it's good. But I kept fixing the same things by hand: radii that don't nest, buttons with no hierarchy, icons from three different sets, motion that's either missing or bouncing for no reason. The same happened with video. The edits were fine, and every one of them looked like every other one.

So I started writing my corrections down as rules, then as skills an agent can load. 21 Grams is that notebook, cleaned up and shared. Every rule in it comes from my own work, from specimens I picked, or from videos and talks I learned from.

Start

How skills work

A skill is a folder with a SKILL.md and the rules, references and scripts it needs. Your agent reads the short description up front and only loads the rest when a request matches, so installing several costs almost nothing.

Skills here are grouped into families. Design skills work on your codebase. Video skills work on footage on your machine. More families will be added as they earn their place.

Installing

Every skill page has its own install command. They all come from the same repo:

/plugin marketplace add ilbognov/21grams

After that, ask in plain words. “Uplift this settings screen” is enough for the agent to pick up 21 Grams UI.

What's inside

  • Rule zeroFixes go through your components and tokens. No new components, no new packages.
  • IconsOne style per group, line, solid or duotone, never mixed across states.
  • Principles20 UX and animation principles, from squash and stretch to Fitts's, Hick's and the Doherty threshold.
  • Hacks10 design hacks: concentric radius, alpha borders, OKLCH colour, dvh and more.
  • Craft20 animation crafts, a typography specimen guide and an unslop pass.
  • LayoutA centred reading column of 600–720px for settings, forms and detail pages, even page padding, and one page header: the parent beside the back arrow, the title below.
  • CardsEntities are cards, peer sections get their own containers, sibling cards align with text clamped to 2 lines, and no tag repeats what the card says.
  • DetailsIcon rows with equal insets, pinned picker headers, signed deltas, switches for binary settings, actions that look clickable, accordion motion and quiet field details.
  • AI contentA light card with a thin outline, where a light runs once around the edge and then rests.
  • MobileOn a phone, meta lines are 14px and titles and buttons 16px. 12px is only for tags, nav group labels and axis ticks.
  • StatsStat cards with a quiet title and the number loudest. Usage meters read “8/12 sessions” in countable segments that go green, amber, then red. Digits roll on hover, only the ones that change.
  • SchedulesA week calendar: times down, days across, block height for duration, a now-line and a faded past. A date strip on phones.
  • VerdictsFull-screen results: a green phone screen that closes itself after 5 seconds, and a grainy glow rising from the bottom on staff screens.
  • Pass cardTilts while held, flips in 3D on tap, and sits in a bright sheet for scanning.
  • PlacementCreate is a top-right button, actions sit on the row they change, and notifications get their own page.
  • Dark modeSecondary buttons are visibly lighter than their card, and cards sit a small step above the page.
  • FieldsInputs respond on the next frame, even when live data updates around them.

21 Grams UX

v1.2 · available

Audits and fixes the flows of products built by AI, so they work the way people expect and don't just look finished. It walks the real tasks, finds the dead ends, missing states, silent failures and actions you can't take back, then fixes them with what your project already has.

Install

npx skills add ilbognov/21grams --skill ux
or, inside Claude Code
/plugin marketplace add ilbognov/21grams
/plugin install 21grams@21grams

Then ask: “run 21 grams UX on the onboarding flow”. With both skills installed, run UX first: there's no point polishing a step that shouldn't exist.

      What's inside

      • Rule zeroFixes go through your router, dialogs, toasts and empty states. No new components, no new packages, no change to what the product does.
      • LawsThe seven core UX laws: Hick's, Fitts's, Jakob's, Miller's, the Doherty threshold, Tesler's and Postel's.
      • RulesConcrete rules for the moments AI gets wrong: X versus back, no blank loading, scroll restoration, optimistic likes, auto-saved drafts, permissions in context, exact deep links.
      • Field rules50 rules learned from real review sessions on AI-built products, from one control per job to delete scopes, AI proposals, onboarding, reading width and N/A for missing data. The set grows after every session.
      • ResearchBefore designing a flow, it looks at how three to five leading products handle it (from Mobbin when connected), goes through them one at a time, then compares and recommends.
      • WorkflowMap the flows, research how leading products do them, walk them in the browser with the edge cases, audit, then fix and walk them again.
